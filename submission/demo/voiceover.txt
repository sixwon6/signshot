This is Signshot, a creative camera for the Solana Seeker. A clean, simple camera for daily life, and a way to memorialize your moments on the blockchain.

It’s a point-and-shoot, with ISO, shutter, colour and focus controls when I want them. The camera comes with a few looks. I’ll pick Disposable. Tap to focus, drag up to brighten, and shoot. What I see in the viewfinder is what I get.

The photo opens right away. It’s kept in Signshot, and Save puts a copy in my phone’s gallery. None of this needs a wallet.

This one I want to publish. I’ll call it After dinner.

An edition of five, at point zero one SOL a copy. The app shows me what it will cost before I approve in the Seeker’s Seed Vault wallet, over Mobile Wallet Adapter. Published. Copy one is mine. This is devnet, so it’s test SOL.

Solana now holds the edition as a Metaplex Core collection: the numbered copies and who owns each one. A public copy of the photo and its details are stored on Arweave.

The publication is also signed by a hardware key inside this Seeker. The Record shows which device signed it, not what was photographed. That evidence is public, so anyone can check it.

To pass it on, I share a card with a QR code, or copy the link. Now a second wallet. I paste the link in Signshot: the photo, copy two of five, and the price. Continue shows the total. Buy, approve, and copy two belongs to this wallet. The price went straight to the creator.

Camera, wallet and a hardware signature, all in one phone: that’s why Signshot is built for Seeker. On mainnet, an edition can also be kept for Seeker Genesis Token holders only.

A camera for every day. Editions for the photos you choose. Signshot.
