Signshot is a utility camera app built for the Solana Seeker. Switch between a simple point-and-shoot and a photographer's toolkit.

In Auto, pick one of four looks, like Disposable or Camcorder, and set its strength. Tap to focus, drag to brighten, and shoot.

Turn the dial to Program or Manual for ISO, shutter, white balance and focus. Any look can be reshaped, down to its grain and glow, and Develop gives a photo a new look later.

The photo opens right away. Save and Share work without a wallet.

This one is worth keeping, so I'll publish it to Solana. I call it Rain study: five copies at point zero one SOL each, with a five percent royalty. I'll keep it for Seeker holders only, so each Seeker Genesis Token can claim one copy.

Continue shows the network cost of publishing, separate from my price, and the device that will sign. Publish sends both transactions to my wallet over Mobile Wallet Adapter, and Seed Vault approves them with one double tap. This is devnet, so it's test SOL.

Published. Solana now holds an edition of five as a Metaplex Core collection, and copy one is mine. Nobody, not even me, can change the photo or the number of copies. A smaller public copy goes to Arweave; the full-size original stays on my phone.

A key inside this Seeker's StrongBox chip signed the publication, so anyone can check which phone and app published it. It doesn't vouch for the scene.

Share sends a link or a QR card. A friend with a Seeker opens it in Signshot, sees the copy number and the price, and approves in their own wallet. The payment comes straight to me. From here I can change the price or send a copy.

Most days it's just my camera. When a moment is worth keeping, the people who were there can hold a numbered copy. Camera, wallet and a hardware signature, all in one Seeker. Signshot.
