FROM YOUR CAMERA TO A COLLECTOR’S WALLET

Shoot it.
Make it collectible.

01

Choose a camera era.

Choose Y2K, Disposable or Camcorder in the live viewfinder, or shoot with Clean. Take the photograph, review it and adjust its look afterward.

Shoot, edit and share images without a wallet.
02

Publish a numbered edition.

Name your photograph, add an optional Moment caption and choose the edition size. Review the public image, details and costs, then approve with your wallet. You keep the first copy within the total.

One copy or multiple numbered copies—you choose.
03

Share and collect.

Share the photograph’s QR code or link. People open its public page, connect a supported wallet, review the fees and approve collection. An available numbered copy is issued to their wallet.

Scanning opens the page. Collecting is your choice.

BUILD AND BROWSE YOUR COLLECTION

Your photographs.
Your collection.

A single image, photographs from an experience or a series of your work. Publish the photographs you choose and collect the ones you want to keep—all from your Seeker.

ALBUMS

Bring photographs together.

Choose an album when publishing each photograph. An album groups the images on a public page, while each photograph keeps its own edition and numbered copies.

COLLECTED & PUBLISHED

A gallery for your photography.

Collected shows the copies in your connected wallet. Published shows the photographs you’ve published, even after your copy has been sent elsewhere. Search your library, filter by album and open a photograph full screen.

SEND A COPY

Pass it on.

Use Send this copy in Photos to transfer a copy you own to another wallet. Sharing the image sends a picture; sending the copy transfers ownership of its NFT and it leaves your wallet.

SEEKER & SOLANA

Your camera and wallet, connected.

Capture, edit, publish and collect on Seeker. Solana records editions and ownership through Metaplex Core. Arweave holds the smaller public photograph and its details. Era Shot brings them together in a gallery built around the photographs.

BEFORE YOU PUBLISH

Plain answers.

Do I need crypto just to take photos?

No. Use the camera, save photos and share images without publishing. Your wallet is needed when you choose an action on Solana.

What does publishing cost?

Whoever signs pays Solana network fees, account rent and the Metaplex Core protocol fee. The app estimates them before approval; your wallet shows the final transaction. Collecting a copy has the same kinds of cost and no purchase price. Storage availability is checked separately.

Can I delete a published photo?

You can delete local files from the app. A public blockchain record and permanent-storage upload cannot be withdrawn through Era Shot. Review the image and details before publishing.

What does owning a copy mean?

You own a numbered photo NFT that can be transferred to another wallet. The photograph remains public; owning a copy does not prevent others from saving the image or grant copyright. Wallets may display copies under unverified collectibles.

Is this proof that a photo is real?

It is proof of a wallet's publication of a particular file. The recorded fingerprints help check exact files. A signature alone does not prove the scene happened, who held the camera, or when the shutter was pressed.

Who can collect a photograph?

The photo page is public. Anyone with access to it can collect while copies remain, one initial claim per wallet. Invite-only collection and private albums are not available.

Can I sell my photographs?

Paid photographer editions are planned. Current editions have no purchase price, although publishing and collecting incur fees.

What is an album?

A public page listing several of your photographs. Each one is still its own edition, collected on its own page at its own edition size; an album issues nothing and has no purchase price. Choose the album when publishing each new photograph. An already-published photograph cannot be moved into an album, and members cannot be removed. An album's name is set when it is created; the app does not offer a rename.
